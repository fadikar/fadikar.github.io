Developing and Deploying a Use-Inspired Metapopulation Modeling Framework for Detailed Tracking of Stratified Health Outcomes

Arindam Fadikar1, Abby Stevens1, Sara Rimer1, Ignacio Martinez-Moyano1, Nicholson Collier1, Chol Mabil2, Emile Jorgensen2, Peter Ruestow2, V. Eloesa McSorley2, Jonathan Ozik1, and Charles Macal1

1Decision and Infrastructure Sciences Division, Argonne National Laboratory, Lemont, IL, USA
2Chicago Department of Public Health, Chicago, IL, USA

Proceedings of the 2025 Winter Simulation Conference (WSC), IEEE, 2025

IEEE Xplore

Abstract

Public health teams often need granular and timely epidemiological insights without the heavy computational overhead of full agent-based simulation. This paper presents MetaRVM, an open-source R package for metapopulation modeling that supports flexible stratification by geography, demographics, and other user-defined subpopulations.

The framework was co-developed with the Chicago Department of Public Health to support operational decision-making. MetaRVM uses structured mixing across subpopulations and an extended SEIR-style disease progression model, enabling richer heterogeneity than homogeneous compartment models while remaining practical for routine use.

The paper demonstrates the framework on influenza in Chicago, including age-stratified outcome tracking and Bayesian optimization based calibration for efficient parameter estimation.
